# Sources

| Claim | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Fire ants (Solenopsis invicta) link bodies into a waterproof raft during floods; it forms in about 100 s | Mlot, Tovey & Hu (2011), "Fire ants self-assemble into waterproof rafts to survive floods", PNAS 108(19) | "about a hundred seconds" in the script |
| Rafts float for days to weeks until reaching land | Mlot et al. 2011; USDA / university extension pages on imported fire ants and flooding | said as "days, even weeks" |
| Hairs on the ant's body trap air; the trapped air keeps the raft afloat and the bottom ants alive | Mlot et al. 2011 | |
| Queen and brood are placed in the middle or top of the raft | Fire-ant flood ecology literature and extension pages (brood used as a buoyant base; queen protected in the middle) | told simply: "ride in the middle, kept dry" |
| Workers move brood upward when soil gets wet | Fire-ant brood relocation by moisture and temperature (extension pages; nest-architecture studies) | |
| Some ants close nest entrances with soil before rain | Behaviour reported for several species (e.g. some harvester ants) | said as "some ants" |
