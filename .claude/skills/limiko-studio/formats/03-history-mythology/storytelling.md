# Storytelling, accuracy and respect

## History: accuracy rules
- Two independent reliable sources per claim (academic books, encyclopaedias, museums, archaeological surveys).
  Write them in `films/<slug>/sources.md`.
- Dates on screen. If historians disagree, say "around" or show the range.
- Label clearly: **fact** (documented), **legend** (traditional account), **theory** (scholarly proposal).
- Don't invent dialogue for real people and present it as a quote. Dramatised lines are fine if
  framed as narration ("He must have wondered…").
- Maps: borders change over time. Draw the map for that year, not modern borders.
- Images: public-domain artworks and photos (pre-1929 works, museum open-access collections) or our own drawings.

## Mythology: respect rules
- Myths are living faith for many people. Tell them as stories from a tradition: "In the Ramayana…",
  "According to the Bhagavata Purana…", "Norse sagas tell…".
- Name the text and version; many myths have several. Mention when versions differ.
- Gods and sacred figures: dignified depiction, traditional colours and attributes
  (e.g. Krishna blue with flute and peacock feather, Hanuman with mace, Shiva with trident and crescent moon).
  No comedy, mockery or redesign that changes their identity.
- No claim that a myth is literal history, and no claim that it's "fake". Present, don't judge.
- Avoid comparing religions as better or worse.
- Thumbnails: no deity shown in a mocking, violent-for-shock or sexualised way.

## Story structure toolkit
| Tool | How |
|---|---|
| Cold open | Start at the most dramatic image, then "but to understand this, we go back <N> years" |
| Question spine | One question carried through the video, answered only at the end |
| Rule of three | Three trials, three warnings, three battles; escalate each |
| The turn | A clear moment where everything changes; mark it with silence then music |
| Echo ending | Final image mirrors the opening image, changed |
| Present-day bridge | End on where the story lives today (a temple, a festival, a word we still use) |

## Narration style
- Short sentences. Strong verbs. One image per sentence.
- Vary pace: slow for awe, quicker for battles, a pause before every reveal.
- Pronounce names correctly. Write a phonetic guide in the script for the voice (e.g. "Kakatiya: KAA-kuh-tee-yuh").

## Checklist before publishing
- [ ] every historical claim in `sources.md`; every myth beat names its text
- [ ] fact / legend / theory labelled where it matters
- [ ] maps and costumes fit the era
- [ ] sacred figures depicted with traditional attributes and dignity
- [ ] music and art match the culture of the story
- [ ] no modern objects or anachronisms in the art
